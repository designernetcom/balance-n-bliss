My Approach to Psychotherapy –

Understanding the whole person, not just the presenting concern.

Every person comes to therapy with a different story. There is no single explanation for why we feel, think or behave the way we do.

My approach brings together psychological, relational, bodily and meaning-centred perspectives to understand what may be happening in your life.

Trauma-informed care

Difficult or overwhelming experiences can influence how we respond to ourselves, other people and the world around us.

A trauma-informed approach prioritises safety, choice, collaboration, dignity and an understanding of how past experiences may shape the present.

Therapy does not require you to disclose everything before you feel ready.

Attachment and relationships

Our significant relationships can shape how we experience closeness, distance, trust, conflict and emotional safety.

We may explore patterns such as withdrawing when overwhelmed, seeking reassurance when uncertain, struggling to express needs or finding it difficult to trust connection.

The aim is not to label you, but to understand what these patterns communicate and what may help you relate differently.

Somatic awareness

Experiences are not only understood through thoughts and words. We may also notice how stress, emotions and relational experiences are felt in the body.

Somatic work may involve developing awareness of bodily sensations, recognising signs of overwhelm and exploring what helps you feel more grounded.

The pace of this work is guided by your needs and capacity.

Cognitive and behavioural approaches

Our thoughts, beliefs and actions influence how we experience difficulties.

Drawing from cognitive and behavioural approaches, we may explore self-critical thinking, assumptions, recurring behaviours and alternative ways of responding to situations.

Solution-focused work

Sometimes it helps to identify existing strengths, exceptions to a problem, useful resources and small steps towards change.

This approach supports a focus on what is possible, not only on what is difficult.

Existential and meaning-centred work

Not every struggle is only about managing symptoms. Sometimes we are also questioning identity, responsibility, purpose, freedom or the direction of our lives.

Meaning-centred work can help us explore what matters, what remains within our influence and what meaningful choices may be possible in our circumstances.

An integrative approach

I do not fit every person into one therapeutic model. We work collaboratively to identify approaches that are appropriate to your concerns, goals, capacities and preferences.

Therapy is not about becoming someone else. It is about developing greater understanding and exploring how you want to live from here.
